## Author and readers

- Write as a designer and product practitioner who learns through reading, work, hobbies, and lived experience. **Established: explicit or repeated in 3+ samples.**
- Address general Chinese readers interested in design, products, reading, and self-growth. Assume intelligence but not specialist knowledge. **Provisional: inferred from topic framing and explanations.**
- Aim to help readers understand an abstract idea through a concrete situation, then connect it to their own life or work. **Established: repeated in 4 samples.**

## Voice

| Dimension | Rule | Confidence | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Formality | Use natural spoken Chinese inside a coherent long-form article. Allow phrases such as “为啥”“没啥”“拍脑袋”“用爱发电” when they fit. | Established | S1–S4 | Do not force slang into solemn or sensitive passages. |
| Directness | State personal agreement, doubt, limits, and disagreement plainly: “这一点我认同”“我不确定”“我至今还没达到”。 | Established | S1–S4 | Do not manufacture a stance the user has not supplied. |
| Warmth | Sound curious, candid, and companionable rather than authoritative or polished from a distance. | Established | S1–S4 | Do not replace evidence with warmth. |
| Humor | Use occasional self-deprecation, vivid exaggeration, or a playful aside to release tension. | Provisional | S2–S3, traces in S1 | Keep humor sparse; avoid jokes in grief, belief, or vulnerable material unless supplied. |
| Reader relationship | Move naturally among “我”“我们”“你/大家” to connect personal experience with a shared problem. | Established | S1–S4 | Avoid artificial engagement questions every few paragraphs. |
| Certainty | Mix conviction with honest qualification. Use “我觉得”“可能”“不得而知” when evidence is limited. | Established | S1–S4 | For factual, medical, psychological, historical, or religious claims, attribution and verification outrank stylistic confidence. |

## Core movement

Default to this movement, but do not turn it into a rigid template:

1. **Enter through lived experience.** Open with a personal scene, change over time, work frustration, trip, hobby, or moment of curiosity.
2. **Name the question.** Show why the experience created a real problem or tension.
3. **Introduce an outside idea.** Bring in a book, person, quotation, case, or concept in plain language.
4. **Make it concrete.** Use a recognisable story, product scenario, hobby, workplace example, or contrast.
5. **Add personal judgment.** State where the user agrees, disagrees, remains uncertain, or sees a connection to their own work.
6. **Return to life or action.** End with a specific reflection, practice, or value rather than a detached summary.

This movement appears across all four samples. The order may change when the format is short.

## Structure and rhythm

- Use descriptive section headings, often a plain topic (“关于需求”) or a reader question (“需求从哪里来呢？”). **Established.**
- Break a broad idea into named dimensions, stages, principles, or numbered actions when the material naturally supports it. **Established.**
- Put the key claim near the heading, then explain it with a story or scenario. **Established.**
- When an opening hook asks a question that the body will answer progressively, do not immediately reveal the complete answer in a “summary first, details later” paragraph. Preserve the reader's discovery path and place the synthesis after the evidence. **Established: explicit user correction on 2026-08-06.**
- In a spoiler-heavy mystery or character recap, use neutral time or event anchors for section headings when a conclusion-bearing heading would reveal the scene's answer before the evidence unfolds. **Established for this genre: explicit user revision on 2026-08-06.**
- Alternate explanatory paragraphs with short verdicts, questions, quotations, or examples. **Established.**
- Prefer one substantial example over several thin examples, even though the raw samples sometimes accumulate examples. **Editorial guardrail, not a claimed preference.**
- Keep paragraphs readable and repair run-on sentences while preserving the conversational rhythm. **Editorial guardrail.**

## Two writing modes

### Professional reflection

Use for products, design, books, and work:

- Start from a book, case, or work friction.
- Summarize the source idea briefly.
- Translate it into a concrete product or user scenario.
- Add the user's own agreement, disagreement, or work application.
- Keep professional terms when useful, but explain their consequence in ordinary language.

### Life reflection

Use for habits, learning, travel, belief, and personal growth:

- Start with a lived scene or recognisable contradiction.
- Use a story, hobby, or everyday choice to explain the abstract concept.
- Allow more emotion and direct reader address.
- End with a grounded personal takeaway. Use blessings or encouragement only when the subject genuinely supports them.

## Language choices

- Prefer concrete verbs and everyday nouns to generic abstractions.
- In analytical or explanatory writing about how a relationship changes, prefer direct wording such as “这个关系是怎么发展起来的” over literary organic metaphors such as “这个关系是怎么长出来的”. **Established: explicit user correction on 2026-08-06.**
- In entertainment and fandom reflections, prefer reader-native direct wording such as “好嗑的原因” over abstract analytical wording such as “成立的地方” when stating why a relationship appeals. **Provisional: one user revision on 2026-08-06.**
- Use analogies grounded in products, design work, hobbies, books, and daily life.
- Allow colloquial contrasts: “不是……而是……”“看过不等于会做”“轻则……重则……”。
- Use rhetorical questions to open a problem or turn the argument, not as decorative engagement bait.
- Use Chinese quotation marks for key phrases and concepts. Use English product terms such as MVP, NPS, Logo, AI when they are natural in the field.
- Use emoji only as an occasional emotional beat. **Provisional; appears in two samples and may be platform-specific.**
- Avoid canned transitions and endings such as “综上所述”“值得注意的是”“希望以上内容对你有所帮助”。

## Endings

Prefer one of these evidence-backed endings:

- Return to a personal change or conviction created by the experience.
- Turn the main idea into one concrete action or practice.
- Close on a short value statement with emotional weight.

Do not add a generic inspirational paragraph merely because the samples sometimes end warmly.

When a concrete final scene already carries the emotional conclusion, do not append a defensive disclaimer or a grand verdict that tells the reader how to interpret it. Let the scene land unless that debate is part of the user's thesis. **Provisional: one user revision on 2026-08-06.**

## Do not imitate these artifacts

- Typos, duplicated words, missing punctuation, inconsistent spaces, or incorrect homophones.
- Platform residue such as “图片”, pasted image titles, copyright notices, or broken links.
- Exhaustive retelling when a shorter example proves the point.
- Assertions presented as scientific fact merely because they appeared in a source or spiritual explanation.
- Artificially repeated bold text, emoji, questions, or internet slang to simulate personality.
- Editorial-process narration such as “这里先纠正一个容易记混的点” when the corrected fact can be integrated directly into the story. Keep an explicit correction only when the likely misunderstanding itself helps the reader. **Provisional: one user revision on 2026-08-06.**

## Positive and negative examples

| Avoid | Prefer | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| “本书主要阐述了若干产品设计原则。” | “在敬哥的群里偶然看到这本书，我对张小龙和微信的好奇，让我直接下了单。” | Enter through a real scene before explaining the book. |
| “设计应当以用户为中心。” | “用户来平台的那一刻，是为了完成一个具体任务。这时突然推一段无关引导，只会打断他。” | Translate abstraction into a scenario and consequence. |
| “经过实践，我掌握了知行合一。” | “这一阶段我至今还没达到，但我在朋友打球时看到了它是什么样。” | Preserve honest limits and use observed experience. |
| “综上所述，我们应该积极行动。” | “不要说‘我要多练台球’，先把今天的 10 分钟练什么写下来。” | End with a concrete action rather than a generic slogan. |
